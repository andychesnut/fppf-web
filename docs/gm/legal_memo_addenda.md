## Addendum 1, 2026-11-18: trademark status (updates memo sections 11 and 13)

**The point.** The "FIRST PRINCIPLES FINANCIAL" application is dead, so it does not block the brand. But the way it died is a new, larger warning: Principal Financial Services, Inc. opposed it. A large financial company polices "Principles" next to financial words.

**Not read:** the notice of opposition itself, so the grounds Principal Financial claimed are unknown. The likely ground is confusion with its PRINCIPAL marks, which is the GM's inference, not a finding. The USPTO status page for either mark would not load without a browser. No maintenance filings were shown for the FPPF registration, and none are due until 2029 to 2030 (GM's reading of the standard six-year rule, unverified).

### What it means

1. **The 2023 application is not a conflict.** It is abandoned. Memo section 11's open lead is closed.
2. **New risk: Principal Financial Services.** It spent money to stop one individual from registering "First Principles Financial" for financial advisory services. "Principles" and "Principal" sound the same. Our name puts "First Principles" next to "Personal Finance" or "Money".
3. **How the risk differs for us.** The opposition was against a registration application for advisory services. We have filed nothing, and we are an education product, not an adviser. An opposition can only happen if we apply to register. A demand letter can happen any time, but is less likely against a small education site than against a class 36 advisory filing. Both statements are the GM's judgment.
4. **The bare acronym decision stands.** The FPPF registration is live in an unrelated field. Keep avoiding "FPPF" in public.

### GM decisions, 2026-11-18

- **Keep the brand and the domain.** One dismissed opposition against a different applicant is not enough evidence to rename before launch.
- **Do not file a trademark application on any "First Principles" name without counsel.** Filing is what invites the opposition.
- **Never use the word "Principal" in the brand, the copy near the name, or ads.** Never describe the product with "financial services", "advisory", or "planning" wording (already rule R4).
- **Cheap insurance: keep the product name separable from the brand.** "Months of No" and "The Snapshot" carry no such risk. If a demand letter ever arrives, the fallback is to rebrand around "Months of No" and keep the product intact. Do not spend on this now.
- **Tripwire.** Any letter, email, or platform notice from Principal Financial or its lawyers halts brand spend and goes to Andy the same day.
- **Counsel scope, question 3, now reads:** trademark clearance on the chosen brand, with specific attention to Principal Financial Services' PRINCIPAL marks and opposition 91290761.

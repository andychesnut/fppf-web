## Who we are

**FPPF (First Principles Personal Finance) is a framework and assessment for aligning personal finances to principles.** Education and self-assessment, never personalized advice.

## The world changed

Most people are living at stage five of financial maturity while their stage one is broken:
- They invest before eliminating high-interest debt.
- They spend before understanding their values.
- They optimize for wealth before establishing stability.
- Obligations pile up one reasonable decision at a time until the person cannot afford to say no.

Nobody ever gave them a map. And the deepest cost is not financial. It is a quiet loss of the freedom to speak, to refuse, and to live fully.

## The studio's goal for this venture (the Principal, 2026-11-18)

**Build a profitable venture that creates positive impact for as many people as possible.** Both halves bind. Profit without impact fails it. Impact that cannot pay for itself fails it.

## Working customer and product thesis (GM decision D-011, 2026-11-18, a hypothesis)

**The customer is the trapped person:** a large gap between how they spend their time, energy, and money and how they want to, with too little resilience to close it. The hero below (the trapped middle manager) is the first segment, because that person has income to redirect and can pay.

**The product's job is to help that person close the gap,** not to grade them. The six F's are the engine under the product, not the thing we sell.

**Known gaps between this thesis and the live product (to fix in order):**
1. The Snapshot never asks what the person wants. It measures resilience but not the gap. Only question 24 touches it.
2. Freedom is scored last, as stage 6. For this customer it is the reason to start. The target comes first; the stages are the path to it.
3. The result names the lowest weak stage. A trapped person needs the first move toward their own exit, tied to their own target.
4. Customer success is defined as a higher six-F score. It should be: the person reports the gap is smaller, or took the step.
5. The paid offer is a report with financial statements. This customer more likely wants a path out: what the life they want costs, and the sequence to get there, as education built on their own inputs (rules R1 to R4 still bind).
6. The headline number ("Months of No") is jargon for a number most people already know.

**Kill rule:** after about 100 real completions, if fewer than 30% say they feel trapped, or fewer than 30% say the result told them something new, drop this thesis and reopen the customer question.

## The hero: the trapped middle manager

Age 32 to 45. Decent income, lifestyle expanded to meet it. Mortgage, car payments, maybe kids, a 401k they contribute to but do not understand. Not broke. Financially trapped.

- **What they want:** to stop feeling that the job owns them. Room to say no, take a risk, be sick or slow or fully human.
- **Their external problem:** obligations outrunning margin, with no honest picture of where they stand.
- **Their internal problem:** the anxiety of not looking, the shame of how it got here, the 2am question of whether this is all there is.
- **The philosophical problem:** people deserve finances built to serve a life, not a life built to serve their finances.

## The stakes

**With the map:** they see their full picture honestly, sequence the next moves correctly, and build margin. **Without it:** the obligations keep compounding, every decision narrows, and the person calculates what they can afford to say before they say it.

## The guide: FPPF

The hero is the person. FPPF is the guide. **Empathy:** we know what it feels like to be too busy and too anxious to look. **Authority:** a structured, evidence-based framework across six stages, scored on what a person has done, not how they feel about themselves.

**The guide's promise: a clear map from where you are to where your finances serve your life, with no shame on the way.**

## The plan

1. Take the free snapshot (Formation and Foundation).
2. See where you stand across the six F's.
3. Get a prioritized action plan built for your situation.

**Success:** seen, clear, and equipped, with a next action. **Failure:** the person stays unseen to themselves. **The transformation:** from reactive and obligated to deliberate and free to choose.

## The mission

**Help people align their personal finances to first principles, so their obligations serve their lives and not the other way around.** *(Proposed wording. The Principal's to confirm.)*

## Why we win

*(Hypothesis. To be tested, not assumed.)*
- **The wedge:** a free, honest snapshot that gives value before any paywall.
- **The moat:** a person's measured progress over time. Re-assessment turns a one-time report into a relationship, and every cycle makes the framework sharper. Privacy rules in the floor limit what is stored to scores and categories.
- **The differentiator:** the framework assesses behavior and evidence, not self-rating. It starts with who a person is, not with their budget.

## Culture - four commitments

*(Proposed, drawn from the Product Brief's principles. The Principal's to confirm or rename.)*

- **Evidence over intention.** We score what people have done. We do the same for ourselves: no unverified claims.
- **Dignity.** We never shame where a person is. We tell them what it means and what it requires.
- **Integrity.** We show our math. We do not mislead, guarantee outcomes, or promise what a framework cannot deliver.
- **Regret minimization.** The goal was never the biggest number. It was a life lived fully and spoken freely. Customer first, business follows.
